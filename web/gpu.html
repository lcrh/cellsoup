          How this ecology works
          

            

              Cells need both nutrients to make energy. They can
              specialize in absorbing A or B; spring links pass nutrients
              between neighbors. Giving energy is an explicit, fractional
              transfer.
            

            

              Food sources wander with a tendency to return toward their home.
              Upkeep can kill a cell even in food if intake cannot cover its
              costs. Voluntary spending preserves a small energy reserve; theft
              can still kill.
            

            

              The archive selects for survival, harvested food and offspring. It
              does not reward forming links. A connected group is a colony, not
              automatically a cooperative organism.
            

            

              The GPU runs the complete lifecycle, including programs, spring
              physics, finite food, birth, death and selection. Rendering uses
              the same GPU memory. Max skips drawings, never physics steps.
            

            

              This experimental model has four links per cell and genomes of at
              most 64 instructions. Genome mutations change an instruction or
              operand. GPU scheduling makes runs with the same seed vary.
            

          

        

        

          Assembly reference
          

            

              Eight registers: r0…r7. Labels, signed numbers and
              register operands. Sensors and movement are relative to the cell.
              Target handles identify current cell slots and can be reused after
              death.
